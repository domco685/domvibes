Act One · 15 min · Read-Only

Find the Money

Somewhere in your order history are customers who bought the same product 2, 3, 4+ times — manually re-ordering on a rhythm. That rhythm is a subscription that doesn't know it exists yet. Let's find out how much GMV it represents in your store.

▶ Do This Now

Paste the prompt below into Claude Code. When it finishes, write down your headline number: total one-time GMV from repeat buyers. We'll compare across the room.

This step is 100% read-only — Claude is querying your order data, not changing anything.

The Opportunity Audit
Analyze my store's order history over the last 12 months and build me a "subscription opportunity audit": 1. Find customers who purchased the same product 2 or more times as one-time purchases (exclude orders that are already subscription orders — Recharge subscription orders are usually tagged "Subscription" or have subscription line-item properties; check both). 2. For each product with repeat one-time buyers, show me: number of repeat buyers, the MEDIAN days between their purchases, and total GMV from those repeat purchases. 3. Rank products by total repeat-purchase GMV. 4. Give me one headline number: the total GMV last year from customers manually re-ordering products. Present it as a table, biggest opportunity first.
📣

Share-out moment: When you have your number, we'll go around the room — biggest one-time GMV pool wins bragging rights. This is the number you'll report to your team on Monday.

Act Two · 15 min · Read-Only

Design the Offer

Here's where the reorder-interval data pays off. If customers rebuy every 34 days, the pitch isn't "10% off" — it's "never run out, delivered every 5 weeks." The data tells you whether this is an incentive problem, a packaging problem, or a reason-to-convert problem.

▶ Do This Now

Pick your #1 product from Step 1. Paste the prompt below and fill in the two blanks — product name and your rough gross margin — then let Claude design the offer.

AI is the analyst here, not just the code generator. Push back on its first answer — ask "why that discount and not less?" and make it defend the math.

The Offer Designer
Using the reorder data from the audit above, design a subscription conversion offer for [PRODUCT NAME]. My gross margin on it is roughly [X]%. 1. Recommend the subscription delivery frequency — match it to the median reorder interval you found, rounded to a natural cadence (30 / 45 / 60 days). 2. Recommend the incentive: subscription discount %, sized so the LTV uplift from converting justifies the margin given up. Show me the math. 3. Consider alternatives to a discount: a bundle/packaging change, a free gift on the 3rd delivery, loyalty points, or early access. Which fits this product best and why? 4. Write the one-sentence conversion pitch a customer would see — lead with the benefit (never run out / set-and-forget), not the discount. Give me a primary recommendation and one alternative.
